Diarrhea

Diarrhea is a condition in which a person will have three or more liquid (or loose) bowel movements per day. The loss of fluids through diarrhea may result in dehydration or electrolyte deficiencies such as salt imbalances.

There are several different types of diarrhea:

Secretory

Secretory diarrhea refers to an increase in active secretion, or an inhibition of absorption. This kind of diarrhea continues even in the absence of oral food intake.

Osmotic

Osmotic diarrhea takes place when too much water has been drawn into the bowels. Ingesting beverages with excessive sugar or salt can draw water from the body into the bowel and cause osmotic diarrhea. However, this type of diarrhea can also be caused by insufficient digestion. Healthy individuals may experience osmotic diarrhea in the presence of too much magnesium, vitamin C, undigested lactose, or excess fructose.

Exudative

Exudative diarrhea is often associated with inflammatory bowel diseases and other severe infections such as food poisoning or E. coli. Exudative diarrhea is characterized by the presence of blood and pus in the stool.

Motility-related

Motility-related diarrhea is caused by the rapid movement of food through the intestines (hypermotility). If food that moves through the gastrointestinal tract too quickly, there is not sufficient time for nutrients and water to be absorbed.

Inflammatory

Inflammatory diarrhea is characterized by a loss of protein-rich fluids and a decreased ability to absorb these lost fluids, usually a result of damage to the mucosal lining. Ailments such as bacterial infections, viral infections, parasitic infections or autoimmune deficiencies can contribute to this type of diarrhea.

Dysentery

Generally, if blood is visible in the stool this indicates dysentery. Dysentery is often a symptom of Shigella, Entamoeba histolytica, and Salmonella, among other things.
